I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at Emory University. My goal is to turn advances in AI into greater human agency: helping people accomplish complex goals while retaining meaningful control. My research centers on LLM agents, alignment, and AI interpretability and safety, with a particular interest in multi-agent collaboration and theory of mind. I explore how agents can understand their environments, reason about their collaborators’ beliefs and intentions, and work together effectively. I am especially interested in agents that adapt to people’s values and preferences and recognize when to act autonomously or seek human input. Alongside these capabilities, I study how to evaluate and explain agent behavior, and uncover risks that emerge through interactions among agents.

Before my Ph.D., I spent two years building and deploying LLM and RAG systems in industry, including AI copilots and decision-support systems. I hold an M.S. in Data Science from Columbia University and a B.A. in Computer Science & Economics from Emory University.

Always happy to talk about LLM agents, alignment, or AI safety — reach out any time.

Research Interests

LLM Agents & Collaboration

How can agents understand their environments and collaborators well enough to plan and work together reliably?

Alignment & Human Agency

How should agents adapt to individual goals and preferences, and decide when human involvement is most valuable?

Interpretability & Safety

How can we explain and safeguard agent behavior, including risks that emerge through shared memory and communication?
